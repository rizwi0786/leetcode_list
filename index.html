How to practise so it actually sticks

  1. Learn the pattern first. Read the idea and template, then solve the first 3 problems of that pattern back to back. Same tool, three uses, and your brain files it.
  2. Give each problem 25 minutes. Stuck after that? Read the hint or solution, understand why it works, close it, and code it yourself from scratch.
  3. Say it before you code it. Write one line: "This is sliding window because the answer is a contiguous substring." Naming the pattern is the skill interviews test.
  4. Star anything you needed help on. Redo starred problems after 1 day, 3 days, and 7 days (spaced repetition). If you solve it cleanly on day 7, unstar it.
  5. Mix it up after Line 2. Each week, solve 5 random problems from finished patterns without looking at the pattern name. This is what turns "I know the patterns" into "I can spot the pattern".
  6. Weekly contest from week 6. Timed pressure is a separate skill. Even solving 2 of 4 is fine early on.

Pace guide: 2–3 problems a day finishes the whole map in about 14–16 weeks. An honest note: no list guarantees 90%, but finishing this map and redoing your starred problems until they're clean covers the patterns behind the vast majority of interview and contest questions up to medium-hard.
